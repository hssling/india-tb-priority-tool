How to read this report

Change in notifications. For each district, the change in the number of TB patients notified from 2022 to 2024 is compared with the change for the State as a whole. A district far from the State's change, given its size, lies outside the funnel. Because districts differ for many ordinary reasons, the limits are widened by an overdispersion factor estimated from the State's own districts.

▲ rose beyond adjusted limits
the district rose more than the State, beyond the 99.8% limits after the adjustment
▼ fell beyond adjusted limits
the district fell more than the State, beyond the 99.8% limits after the adjustment
● within adjusted limits
the district's change is in line with the State's

National district module (2018 data). For two steps of the care pathway, the tool asks how many patients would not have been lost if a district's loss rate were as good as the benchmark (the better quarter of all districts): the harm averted. P(top-20) is the share of 10,000 simulations in which the district is among the 20 with the largest harm averted at that step; the tier is set from it:

A = act now
P(top-20) ≥ 0.80
P = probable priority
0.50 ≤ P < 0.80
U = uncertain
0.20 ≤ P < 0.50
L = lower priority
P < 0.20

the district loses a larger share of patients than its State, beyond the 99.8% limits after the adjustment

Caveats

  • A fall in notifications can mean less TB or less case-finding, and a rise can mean more TB or more case-finding. Notifications alone cannot tell these apart.
  • The notification funnel has no tiers: a flag marks a change beyond what the State's own districts make usual, not a ranking or a judgement of any district.
  • The overdispersion adjustment (phi) is estimated within each State; when phi is well above 1 the limits are wide and few districts are flagged. The additive (tau²) adjustment is shown alongside.
  • Lok Sabha Question 802 (answered 7 February 2025) prints Madhya Pradesh's district "Vidisha" twice with identical values; it is kept once and flagged. The three Madhya Pradesh districts created in 2023 are not printed and are not added. Districts renamed or split since 2018 are not linked to the 2018 table.
  • The national district results are 2018 data (historical), from the India TB Report 2019. Many districts have since been renamed or split; the results are not linked to current districts and are a demonstration of method, not current priorities.
  • D1 compares patients notified by the diagnosing facility in the district with patients initiated on treatment in the district (ITR 2019 Annexure 1.13 footnote). Patients diagnosed in one district and treated in another (for example at referral centres in large cities) count as not initiated in the first.
  • D2's cohort size is rebuilt from the printed success count and a rounded percentage; each value is flagged rounded_from_percent and carries the range implied by the rounding (outputs/district_national_2018.csv).
  • With denominators of thousands of patients, P(top-20) mostly reflects binomial noise and is close to 0 or 1; the tiers express little uncertainty beyond sampling.
  • Harm averted is an absolute count, so large districts have large values. It describes programme data, not the performance or effort of any district's staff, and is not a causal estimate.
